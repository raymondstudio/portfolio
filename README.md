## About me

I'm Raymond, a Software Engineering student at the Federal University of Technology, Minna and a full-stack developer focused on building practical digital products.

I started with the frontend because I liked seeing ideas become interfaces. Over time, that turned into a much broader interest in how products work behind the screen: APIs, databases, authentication, AI integrations, deployment, search, and the decisions that make an application feel reliable rather than just functional.

These days, I spend most of my time building with JavaScript and TypeScript, experimenting with AI-powered products, and turning rough ideas into working applications.

My work usually sits somewhere between **software engineering, product design, and AI**.

> I care about the part after "it works": making the product clear, responsive, maintainable, and worth using.

### EduAgent AI

<a href="https://eduagentai.vercel.app">
  <img src="./assets/images/EDUAGENT AI BANNER.jpg" alt="EduAgent AI preview" width="100%" />
</a>

**EduAgent AI** is an AI-powered study companion built around the way students actually study.

Students can upload lecture slides, PDFs, screenshots, notes, and other study materials, then interact with an AI tutor that can explain difficult concepts, answer questions, summarize material, and help structure revision.

The project started from a simple problem:

> Students have plenty of study material. The difficult part is knowing what to do with it.

EduAgent turns that material into something students can interact with.

**Core capabilities**

* Upload and process study materials
* AI-powered tutoring and explanations
* Context-aware conversations
* Study material search
* Persistent chat sessions
* AI-generated summaries and notes
* Screenshot and image-based learning
* Study planning and revision support
* Authentication and user workspaces

**Stack**

`Next.js` `React` `TypeScript` `Node.js` `Supabase` `Gemini API` `Elasticsearch` `Tailwind CSS` `Vercel`

* [Live application](https://eduagentai.vercel.app)

### Atrixia

<a href="#">
  <img src=".//assets/images/atrixia_cover.png" alt="Atrixia preview" width="100%" />
</a>

**Atrixia** is an AI-assisted shopping decision platform designed to help people make better purchasing decisions instead of simply showing them more products.

The idea came from a familiar situation:

> "I need a laptop for software engineering. My budget is ₦500,000. What should I buy?"

Instead of forcing someone to compare dozens of specifications, Atrixia focuses on the actual decision: understanding the user's needs, budget, priorities, and constraints, then helping them narrow down the right options.

The goal is to make product research feel more like having a knowledgeable person help you shop.

**The product explores**

* Natural-language product discovery
* AI-assisted recommendations
* Budget-aware decision making
* Product comparison
* User preferences and constraints
* Search and retrieval
* Structured product information
* AI-generated explanations

**Stack**

`Next.js` `React` `TypeScript` `Node.js` `AI APIs` `Search` `Tailwind CSS`

## How I approach building products

I usually think about a product in four layers.

```text
                    PRODUCT
                       │
          ┌────────────┼────────────┐
          │            │            │
          ▼            ▼            ▼
         UX         ENGINEERING      AI
          │            │            │
          │            │            │
          └────────────┼────────────┘
                       ▼
                    DELIVERY
```

### 01. Understand the problem

Before writing code, I want to know what the user is actually trying to accomplish.

A good interface cannot rescue a product that solves the wrong problem.

### 02. Build the experience

I care about hierarchy, responsiveness, interaction, accessibility, and the small details users notice even when they cannot explain why.

### 03. Make the system work

That means APIs, authentication, databases, search, state management, error handling, AI integrations, and everything else behind the interface.

### 04. Ship and improve

A project is much more useful when people can actually use it.

I prefer getting a working version in front of users, learning from it, and improving the product rather than spending forever polishing something nobody has tried.
